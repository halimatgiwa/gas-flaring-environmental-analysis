🌍 Environmental Impact of the Petroleum Industry: Gas Flaring, Emissions and Sustainability

📌 Project Overview

This project was developed as my final Data Analytics Capstone Project during my Data Analytics Internship at AnalystLab Africa.

The project explores gas flaring within the petroleum industry, focusing on global flaring patterns, Nigeria’s petroleum-related flaring activities, and changes in flaring across countries.

The project allowed me to combine my academic background in Petroleum Chemistry with my growing technical skills in Data Analytics, particularly Power BI, SQL, Excel and Python.

Interestingly, the topic builds on an area I previously explored during my final-year seminar in Petroleum Chemistry, which I revisited from a data analytics perspective.

⸻

🎯 Project Objective

The main objective of this project is to analyze gas-flaring data and transform it into meaningful insights that can support environmental monitoring and more sustainable petroleum operations.

Key objectives

* Analyze global gas-flaring patterns from 2012–2025.
* Examine flare activities across countries.
* Analyze Nigeria’s petroleum-related flare activities.
* Examine flare records across operators, fields and field types.
* Identify changes in flare volume.
* Identify countries showing reductions in flaring.
* Develop interactive dashboards for data-driven environmental analysis.
* Provide recommendations for reducing the environmental impact of gas flaring.

⸻

❓ Key Questions

The analysis was guided by questions such as:

1. How has gas flaring changed over the study period?
2. Which countries have significant flare activities?
3. What does Nigeria’s flare activity look like within the dataset?
4. How are flare activities distributed across operators and petroleum fields?
5. What changes in flare volume can be observed?
6. Which countries are identified as reducing flaring?
7. What strategies can support the reduction of gas flaring?

💡 Key Insights

The analysis revealed several important observations:

* Gas flaring is a global petroleum-industry issue, with the dataset covering 104 countries.
* Approximately 27K flare locations are represented in the analysis.
* Nigeria’s records include 465 flare records across 47 operators and 192 flare fields.
* The dashboard identifies 45 countries reducing flaring.
* Operator, field and geographical information can support more targeted environmental monitoring.
* Data visualization makes complex flaring information easier to interpret and communicate.

⸻

💡 Recommendations

Based on the analysis, the project recommends:

1. Increase gas capture and utilization instead of routine flaring where technically feasible.
2. Strengthen flare-reduction targets and regularly monitor performance.
3. Promote gas recovery and reinjection where appropriate.
4. Improve leak detection and methane management around petroleum facilities.
5. Use data-driven monitoring to identify assets requiring environmental attention.
6. Study successful flare-reduction practices from countries showing improvements.
7. Strengthen environmental monitoring and remediation where petroleum activities have resulted in contamination.

⸻

⚠️ Limitations

The analysis has some limitations:

* The dataset focuses primarily on gas-flaring activities and related petroleum assets.
* It does not directly measure every environmental impact associated with petroleum production.
* Direct causal relationships between petroleum production volumes and gas flaring cannot be established from the available working dataset.
* Direct flaring-intensity comparisons are limited by the variables available in the dataset.

Therefore, the findings should be interpreted as data-driven observations rather than causal conclusions.
